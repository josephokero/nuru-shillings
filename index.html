Why Nora Shillings

Capability Nora Shillings Typical processor
Payment methodsNora balance, M-Pesa, PayPal, and cards, on one checkoutUsually one method, and the others are someone else’s problem

A buyer is not forced onto a single rail. If they already hold Nora Shillings, they spend that balance. If they do not, they pay with M-Pesa, PayPal, or a card without leaving the page. The merchant keeps one checkout instead of stitching separate processors together.

Who can payAny buyer with a wallet, or with M-Pesa, PayPal, or a cardOnly customers that processor already has

There is no extra account the buyer must open on the merchant’s site. A person with Nora Shillings pays from that wallet. A person without it still pays, using M-Pesa, PayPal, or a card. The merchant reaches both.

SettlementPrivate chain. Nothing is posted in publicA ledger the processor’s network can see

The payment is settled on Nora’s private chain. It is not written to a public network where anyone can follow the amount, the time, and the path. The company is the name on the payment. The person behind it is not.

Checkout displayThe company name. Not the personThe buyer’s identity is often attached to the charge

What a checkout shows is the company taking the payment. It does not put the buyer’s name on display as the public face of the transaction. The merchant still receives a clear paid or refused result, with their own order reference.

DepositsIn the wallet immediatelyOften held for review, then paid out later

Money added to the wallet is available at once, at 100 Nora Shillings to 1 US dollar. The buyer does not wait through a hold before they can spend. A later payment draws on that balance the moment they confirm.

Published rate100 NSH = 1 USD, on every siteThe rate and the fee change by corridor

The unit does not get renegotiated at checkout. Ten dollars is 1,000 NSH. Eighteen dollars is 1,800 NSH. A shop in one country and a buyer in another use that same rate, so the amount on the page is the amount that moves.

RecordKept on Nora’s own chainCopied onto a public or partner ledger

The payment record stays with Nora. It carries the amount, the unit, the merchant’s reference, and the status. It is not left on a public ledger where a third party can trace it from the outside.

Merchant categoryShops, apps, and adult sites are acceptedAdult sites and similar categories are refused

Card processors decline whole categories, including adult content. Nora does not refuse a site for that reason. A shop, an app, a marketplace, or an adult site can take payment on its own page, and the buyer still pays with Nora, M-Pesa, PayPal, or a card.

Checkout locationThe buyer stays on the merchant’s pageThe buyer is often sent to another site

The order, the customer, and the page remain the merchant’s. Nora is the way the buyer pays there. They do not create a second account, and they are not moved to a hosted page that belongs to someone else.

RefundReturned to the same wallet, by payment idReturned on the original rail, if that rail allows it

A refund uses the payment id. The Nora Shillings go back to the same wallet, and the activity shows the return next to the original charge. The merchant matches it with the reference they sent when the payment was created.

01 — The coin

A single unit you can hold and spend.

Nora Shillings are not a new account on every website. They are one balance. Platforms connect that balance as a way to pay.

Bought in one place

Add money by card or bank transfer. The wallet credits Nora Shillings at the published rate and shows the dollar value beside the balance.

Priced the same everywhere

A platform in one country and a buyer in another use the same unit. Nobody renegotiates what a Nora Shilling is at the moment of payment.

Spent where it is accepted

If a shop, app, or marketplace has connected the API, the buyer can pay with the coin they already hold. The rest of checkout stays theirs.

05 — Checkout

Five steps. The buyer stays put.

The platform keeps its page, its order, and its customer. Nora Shillings is a way to pay on that page, not a second website the buyer must join.

  1. 01

    The buyer holds a balance

    They add money once. The wallet shows Nora Shillings and the dollar equivalent. That balance is what later checkouts draw from.

  2. 02

    The platform creates a payment

    It sends the amount in Nora Shillings, the unit, and its own order reference. The reference is how the platform matches our result to its order.

  3. 03

    The buyer confirms on that site

    They see the amount and the platform name where they are already shopping. They do not create a new account for that shop inside Nora Shillings.

  4. 04

    The balance moves

    If the wallet can cover the amount, it is debited and the payment is marked paid. If it cannot, the payment is refused and nothing moves.

  5. 05

    The platform is told at once

    It receives the payment id, the status, and the same reference it sent. A refund uses that id and returns the Nora Shillings to the buyer.

06 — Two sides

What each person actually does.

The buyer

Holds the coin and confirms.

  • Opens one wallet, in any country.
  • Adds money and sees both NSH and USD.
  • Pays a platform without leaving its checkout.
  • Reads every add and every payment in one list.

The platform

Connects the API and gets a yes or no.

  • Keeps its own customers and its own page.
  • Prices the order in Nora Shillings.
  • Sends amount, unit, and an order reference.
  • Marks the order from the status we return.

07 — For platforms

A payment method, not a new accounts system.

You do not import your users into Nora Shillings. You offer the coin beside the ways you already take payment. We confirm the balance and tell you the result.

Your checkout

The button lives on your page. Your order number stays your order number. We store it as the reference and send it back with the result.

A clear result

Paid, or refused. You do not infer success from a redirect. The payment object carries the status, the amount, and your reference.

A refund on the same rail

Refund against the payment id. The Nora Shillings return to the buyer’s wallet, and the payment records the refund beside the original charge.

08 — The API

Three calls. One payment.

Create the payment, read it back, refund it. Amounts are strings in Nora Shillings so a platform does not lose cents to floating point.

POST/v1/payments

Open a charge for an order.

GET/v1/payments/:id

Read status and reference.

POST/v1/refunds

09 — Where it fits

Any place that already takes a payment.

Shops

A catalog price is shown in dollars and charged in Nora Shillings. The order is fulfilled when the status is paid.

Subscriptions

Each period is a new payment with its own reference. A failed period is a refused payment, not a silent gap.

Marketplaces

The marketplace is the platform. It creates the payment, then settles with its sellers from the result.

Apps

An app sells a feature or a credit pack. The buyer confirms the Nora Shilling amount inside the app.

Invoices

An invoice number is the reference. When it is paid, the same number comes back on the payment.

Refunds

A returned order is a refund call. The buyer sees the Nora Shillings back in the wallet activity.

10 — The record

Every payment carries the same fields.

Nothing important is left in a note. The platform can reconcile an order from the object alone.

id

The payment’s own id. Refunds point at this.

amount

The charge in Nora Shillings, as a string.

unit

Always NSH, so a client cannot mix currencies.

reference

The platform’s order, invoice, or subscription id.

status

Paid or refused. The platform branches on this.

created

When the payment was decided, for the ledger.

11 — Questions

Short answers.

Is this only for one country?

No. A buyer can live in one country and a platform in another. The coin is priced in US dollars so the unit does not change at the border.

What is a Nora Shilling worth?

100 Nora Shillings equal 1 US dollar. Ten dollars buys 1,000 NSH. Eighteen dollars is a charge of 1,800 NSH.

Does the buyer leave the platform to pay?

No. Checkout stays on the platform. The platform asks us whether the balance covers the amount, and the buyer confirms there.

Is this a public blockchain?

No. It is a wallet and a payment API. Platforms integrate it the way they integrate any other way to pay.

What if the balance is too low?

The payment is refused. The balance is left as it was, and the platform receives a refused status instead of paid.

Can a platform refund?

Yes. It refunds by payment id. The Nora Shillings go back to the same wallet, and the activity shows the return.
